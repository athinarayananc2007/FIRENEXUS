1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation

1.1 Project Overview & Mission

FIRENEXUS is an enterprise-grade global satellite active fire observation, harmonization, and scientific intelligence platform. It bridges the observational divergence between NASA's flagship Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) instruments aboard the Terra and Aqua spacecraft and the Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS) instruments aboard the Suomi-NPP, NOAA-20, and NOAA-21 spacecraft.

The platform ingests live global active fire telemetry from NASA's Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS), applies physical footprint geometry modeling to account for off-nadir pixel expansion, aligns observations using orbit-aware overpass windows, clusters individual hotspot pixels into multi-day wildfire complexes, and performs rigorous empirical calibration and anomaly detection.

1.2 The Global Wildfire Crisis

Wildfires across the boreal forests of Canada and Siberia, the tropical peatlands of Southeast Asia, the Amazon basin, the Australian bush, and the Mediterranean have broken historical records in burned area, intensity, and duration. Catastrophic fire events inject gigatons of carbon dioxide into the atmosphere, generate hazardous particulate matter (PM2.5) plumes that travel thousands of kilometers, and deplete emergency suppression resources. Because massive wildfires occur in remote wildernesses where ground monitoring is physically impossible, orbital remote sensing is humanity's sole mechanism for continuous planetary surveillance.

1.3 The Core Satellite Observation Dilemma

Wildfire response and Earth system modeling rely primarily on two polar-orbiting satellite sensor families. However, direct concatenation of their raw observation catalogs is fundamentally invalid due to four physical and orbital discrepancies:

  • Spatial Resolution Mismatch: MODIS operates at 1,000 m (1.0 km) nadir resolution, expanding to ~4.8 km along-scan × 2.0 km along-track (~9.6 km²) at swath edges. VIIRS operates at 375 m nadir resolution, constrained by onboard 3:1 sample aggregation to ~0.8 km × 0.8 km (~0.64 km²) at swath edges. A single MODIS pixel covers over 7 times the ground area of a VIIRS pixel at nadir, and over 15 times the area at swath edge.
  • Detection Sensitivity Floor: VIIRS can detect sub-pixel active fires releasing as little as 0.5 to 1.0 MW of Fire Radiative Power (FRP). MODIS typically requires 8.0 to 11.0 MW to trigger a detection. Consequently, VIIRS detects 3× to 5× more active fires globally than MODIS.
  • Diurnal Sampling Bias: Terra overpasses the equator at 10:30 AM (morning descending pass), capturing early burning conditions. Aqua and VIIRS overpass at approximately 1:30 PM (afternoon ascending pass), coinciding with daily minimum relative humidity, peak solar heating, and maximum fire activity.
  • Radiometric Saturation Differences: VIIRS 375 m Band I4 saturates at approximately 367 Kelvin, leading to radiance clipping over extreme wildfire fronts. MODIS Band 21 is a dedicated low-gain channel that remains linear up to 500 Kelvin.
Scientific Consequence of Unharmonized Data: Simply merging MODIS and VIIRS tables in a database produces artificial step-function jumps in long-term fire trend models, distorts median fire radiative power downward, and generates duplicate, contradictory hotspot markers for emergency responders on the ground.

2. Theoretical Foundations & Sensor Physics

2.1 Physical Principles of Active Fire Detection & FRP

Active fire detection relies on thermal radiation emission governed by Planck's Law. Flaming wildland fires burn between 800 K and 1,200 K, shifting their peak blackbody radiant emittance into the mid-infrared (MIR) atmospheric window between 3.5 µm and 4.0 µm. Non-burning ambient Earth surfaces (280–310 K) emit primarily in the longwave thermal infrared (TIR) window around 10–12 µm.

By measuring the sharp radiance anomaly in the 4 µm channel ($L_4$) relative to the background 11 µm channel ($L_{11}$), satellite algorithms detect active combustion even when the fire occupies less than 0.1% of the pixel's ground surface area.

Wooster's Fire Radiative Power (FRP) Approximation:
$$\text{FRP} = \frac{A_{\text{pixel}} \cdot \sigma}{a} \left( L_{4} - \overline{L_{4, \text{background}}} \right) \quad \text{[Megawatts, MW]}$$

Where $A_{\text{pixel}}$ is the ground pixel area, $\sigma$ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant ($5.6704 \times 10^{-8} \text{ W m}^{-2} \text{ K}^{-4}$), and $a$ is a sensor-specific empirical tuning constant. Because biomass combustion rate is linearly proportional to radiant energy release ($\approx 0.368 \pm 0.015 \text{ kg}$ dry biomass per Megajoule), FRP provides a direct measure of fuel consumption and carbon emission rate.

3. System Architecture & Component Decomposition

3.1 Four-Tier Geospatial Architecture

FIRENEXUS is engineered as a decoupled four-tier geospatial architecture adhering to cloud-native microservice principles:

  • Tier 1: Decoupled Presentation Layer (React 19 + TypeScript + Vite): Client application delivering 9 specialized workspaces. Incorporates hardware-accelerated WebGL Three.js for 3D planetary rendering, Leaflet for 2D mapping, and Recharts for dynamic analytics. Features an intelligent API adapter layer (`apiClient.ts`) that transparently falls back to cached baseline datasets if the backend is unreachable.
  • Tier 2: Application & API Layer (Python Flask 3.1): Modular application factory exposing 26 versioned REST endpoints under `/api/v1/*`. Implements pure scientific algorithms in a decoupled service layer, isolated database queries in a repository layer, and standard RFC 7807 JSON error responses.
  • Tier 3: Geospatial Database Layer (PostgreSQL 16 + PostGIS 3.4): Relational object-relational database with native Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC) Simple Features support. Employs WGS 84 SRID 4326 point geometries and Generalized Search Tree (GiST) R-Tree spatial indexing for sub-millisecond proximity queries across 14 tables.
  • Tier 4: Background Processing Layer (Python CLI & Scheduled Workers): Autonomous ingestion, harmonization, and anomaly detection orchestrator (`run_background_pipeline.py`) utilizing process-level lockfiles (`.pipeline.lock`) to enforce rate limits and prevent concurrency deadlocks.

4. End-to-End Scientific Processing Workflow

FIRENEXUS transforms raw planetary satellite observations into harmonized intelligence through an automated 12-stage scientific data pipeline:

Stage 1: NASA FIRMS Ingestion

The backend connects to the NASA EOSDIS MODAPS REST API (`https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/api`). The `FirmsApiClient` manages authenticated requests using the server-side `FIRMS_MAP_KEY`. To honor NASA's strict query constraints (maximum 5-day span per area request), the `IngestionService` chunks multi-day queries into 5-day intervals, monitors HTTP 429 rate-limiting response headers, and applies an exponential backoff factor of 1.5× across 3 retry attempts. All 8 operational product codes are supported:

  • MODIS_NRT & MODIS_SP: Terra & Aqua Near-Real-Time and Standard Processing (Collection 6.1).
  • VIIRS_SNPP_NRT & VIIRS_SNPP_SP: Suomi-NPP 375 m Near-Real-Time and Standard Processing.
  • VIIRS_NOAA20_NRT & VIIRS_NOAA20_SP: NOAA-20 (JPSS-1) 375 m products.
  • VIIRS_NOAA21_NRT & VIIRS_NOAA21_SP: NOAA-21 (JPSS-2) 375 m products.

Stage 2: Streaming Validation & Cryptographic Deduplication

Raw CSV streams are processed line-by-line using `FirmsCsvParser` to maintain a flat memory footprint. The parser validates latitude $[-90.0, +90.0]$, longitude $[-180.0, +180.0]$, normalizes timestamps to UTC ISO-8601, maps confidence, and constructs an immutable SHA-256 fingerprint:

$$\text{dedup\_hash} = \operatorname{SHA-256}\left( \text{source} \parallel \text{satellite} \parallel \text{acq\_datetime} \parallel \text{lat} \parallel \text{lon} \parallel \text{frp} \right)$$

In `ObservationRepository.bulk_insert_observations()`, incoming hashes are queried against existing records. Duplicates are discarded before SQL execution, guaranteeing 100% idempotent storage.

Stage 3: Footprint Geometry Modeling & Sensor Normalization

To move beyond treating satellite detections as dimensionless points, `SensorNormalizationService` calculates ground footprint dimensions based on scan and track enlargement. The effective circular radius is computed as $r = \frac{\sqrt{S \cdot T}}{2} \cdot 1000\text{ m}$. Incompatible confidence metrics are standardized: MODIS continuous integers are mapped to discrete categories ($<30\%$ low, $30\%–80\%$ nominal, $>80\%$ high) to align with VIIRS.

Stage 4 & 5: Dual-Component Spatiotemporal Matching

To eliminate the $O(N \times M)$ computational bottleneck, observations are partitioned into coarse spatial bins ($0.05^\circ \approx 5.5\text{ km}$). For each MODIS hotspot, the engine queries only the $3 \times 3$ adjacent grid neighborhood.

Spatial distance is evaluated using the geodesic Haversine formula ($R = 6,371,000\text{ m}$). Footprint intersection ratio $\eta \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is calculated: if a smaller VIIRS pixel falls entirely within the larger MODIS pixel, $\eta = 1.0$. Temporal windows are orbit-aware: afternoon overpasses (Aqua $\leftrightarrow$ VIIRS) are evaluated within a strict 3,600-second (1-hour) window; morning-to-afternoon passes (Terra $\leftrightarrow$ VIIRS) use a 14,400-second (4-hour) diurnal window.

Stage 6: Composite Harmonization Scoring

Normalized spatial proximity and temporal alignment are combined into an empirical correspondence score:

$$s_{\text{spatial}} = \max\left(0, 1 - \frac{d}{2000\text{ m}}\right), \quad s_{\text{temporal}} = \max\left(0, 1 - \frac{\Delta t}{w}\right)$$ $$\text{match\_score} = 0.60 \cdot s_{\text{spatial}} + 0.40 \cdot s_{\text{temporal}}$$

Pairs with $\text{score} \ge 0.70$ are classified as confirmed matches; scores between 0.25 and 0.70 are tagged as candidate associations.

Stage 7: Connected-Component Fire Event Clustering

In nature, contiguous satellite hotspots represent a single flaming fire front. `FireEventService` executes connected-component clustering. Observations within $3.0\text{ km}$ and $12\text{ hours}$ are grouped into a `FireEvent`. To prevent runaway transitive chaining across vast geographic lines, the algorithm enforces a maximum diameter constraint: no cluster member may exceed $15.0\text{ km}$ from the cluster seed. The cluster computes multi-sensor centroids, total and peak FRP, duration in hours, and spatial radius.

Stage 8 & 9: FRP Statistical Analytics & Calibration

`FRPAnalysisService` computes parametric metrics (mean, variance, standard deviation) and robust non-parametric metrics (median, percentiles $P_{10}$ to $P_{99}$, IQR). On matched pairs, it evaluates Mean Bias Error (MBE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Pearson correlation ($r$), and Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$). `CalibrationService` fits Ordinary Least Squares ($y = \beta_1 x + \beta_0$) and zero-intercept ratio models ($y = \beta x$) using a strict chronological 70/30 train/test split, preventing temporal data leakage.

Stage 10: Multi-Method Anomaly Detection

`AnomalyDetectionService` inspects thermal anomaly catalogs across five distinct methods: standard Z-scores ($Z \ge 3.0$), Modified Z-scores using Median Absolute Deviation ($M_i \ge 3.5$), Interquartile Range upper fences ($Q_3 + 1.5 \cdot \text{IQR}$), mid-infrared physical channel saturation limits (VIIRS $\ge 365\text{ K}$, MODIS $\ge 450\text{ K}$), and spatial isolation tests ($d_{\min} > 15\text{ km}$). Flagged anomalies are cataloged without modifying or deleting raw observations.

Stage 11 & 12: Analytics Aggregation & Multi-Workspace UI

Aggregated metrics are compiled into daily, weekly, and monthly activity summaries and 24-hour UTC diurnal distribution curves. The frontend visualizes this intelligence across 9 specialized workspaces.

5.2 WebGL 3D Planetary Globe Implementation

In `HeroGlobe.tsx` and `GlobeView.tsx`, the 3D Earth globe is rendered using Three.js and `@react-three/fiber` at 60 FPS:

  • Geographic Coordinate Mapping: Converts spherical latitude and longitude (decimal degrees) into 3D Cartesian coordinates on a sphere of radius $R$:
    $$\phi = (90^\circ - \text{lat}) \cdot \frac{\pi}{180^\circ}, \quad \theta = (\text{lon} + 180^\circ) \cdot \frac{\pi}{180^\circ}$$ $$x = -(R \cdot \sin\phi \cdot \cos\theta), \quad y = R \cdot \cos\phi, \quad z = R \cdot \sin\phi \cdot \sin\theta$$
  • Local High-Resolution Textures: Loads NASA Blue Marble imagery locally from `/textures/` (`earth_day.jpg`, `earth_normal.jpg`, `earth_specular.jpg`, `earth_clouds.png`, `earth_lights.png`).
  • Atmospheric Shading & Clouds: Features a custom Fresnel atmospheric limb glow shader and an independent cloud mesh rotating at a differential angular velocity ($\Delta\omega = +0.015\text{ rad/s}$) relative to the Earth sphere.
  • Unified Matrix Transformation: Hotspot markers and the Earth sphere belong to a shared parent `THREE.Group`, ensuring zero marker drift during user pan, tilt, and zoom operations.

9. Security, Operational Reliability & Strategic Roadmap

9.1 Enterprise Security Architecture

  • Zero Client Secret Exposure: The `FIRMS_MAP_KEY` and database `DATABASE_URL` exist exclusively on the server in `backend/.env`. Bundled frontend static assets were inspected to verify zero credential leakage.
  • Protected Administrative Endpoints: Data ingestion, harmonization runs, and calibration training endpoints enforce `ADMIN_API_KEY` verification via `X-Admin-Key` or `Authorization: Bearer ` headers.
  • CORS Restraint: Cross-Origin Resource Sharing is pinned to authorized staging frontend domains (`https://staging.firenexus.org`, `http://localhost:5173`).
  • SQL Injection Immunity: All database interactions utilize SQLAlchemy parameterized queries and GeoAlchemy2 spatial type binders, preventing SQL injection vulnerabilities.

9.2 Five Strategic Roadmap Milestones

  1. Milestone 1: Managed Cloud Database & Continuous Ingestion: Deploy serverless PostgreSQL 16 with PostGIS on Neon or Supabase; activate continuous 6-hour background ingestion via `run_background_pipeline.py`.
  2. Milestone 2: Geostationary Satellite Integration: Ingest NASA/NOAA GOES-16/18 ABI and JMA Himawari-9 active fire products to provide continuous 10-minute diurnal fire tracking between polar overpasses.
  3. Milestone 3: Machine Learning Fire Spread & Smoke Modeling: Couple harmonized Fire Events with NOAA HRRR real-time wind and weather models to forecast fire front propagation and smoke plume injection height.
  4. Milestone 4: Multi-Scale CubeSat & Airborne Verification: Validate empirical calibration models against independent 30 m Landsat 8/9 OLI and 20 m Sentinel-2 MSI active fire and burn scar perimeters.
  5. Milestone 5: Emergency Dispatch & Common Alerting Protocol (CAP): Implement automated webhook and SMS dispatch to emergency services when an active fire event exceeds critical FRP thresholds.
